# Commitment & Revelation Economy

## Overview

The **Commitment & Revelation Economy** is a business and participation model for the Trilema Ecosystem by LAEV.

It connects creative content with cryptographic commitments, participant discovery, controlled revelation, verification, and benefits. The model is designed so that information contained in one work, edition, platform, campaign, or collectible can become relevant to another part of the ecosystem.

The purpose is not simply to hide information. The purpose is to create a documented economic relationship between **content and participation**.

## Core Sequence

The canonical business sequence is:

**Creation → Participation → Commitment → Discovery → Revelation → Verification → Benefit → Redemption**

In operational terms:

**content → participation → cryptography → discovery → revelation → verification → benefits → redemption**

Each stage can be implemented independently while remaining connected to the same campaign or ecosystem event.

---

## 1. Creation

A creative work, campaign, collectible, publication, song, comic, digital artifact, or platform-native experience is created.

The content may contain or be associated with:

- a commitment;
- an identifier;
- a serial;
- a campaign reference;
- a future revelation condition;
- a benefit condition;
- a verification method.

The commitment may be visible, partially visible, or intentionally undisclosed.

---

## 2. Participation

A person participates by interacting with the ecosystem.

Participation may include:

- reading or viewing content;
- acquiring an edition or collectible;
- following a campaign;
- visiting a participating platform;
- identifying a clue;
- combining information from different works;
- submitting a revelation;
- verifying a valid result.

Participation can therefore be passive, exploratory, competitive, collaborative, or transactional depending on the campaign specification.

---

## 3. Commitment

A **commitment** establishes information or a condition before its complete meaning is revealed.

A commitment can be associated with:

- text;
- images;
- panels;
- characters;
- songs;
- editions;
- serial numbers;
- campaign events;
- platform-specific content;
- collectibles;
- external references.

The commitment may use cryptographic primitives such as hashing, nonces, timestamps, or other mechanisms defined by the cryptographic infrastructure.

The business model does not prescribe one cryptographic implementation for every campaign. Technical verification belongs to the canonical **Cryptographic Infrastructure** documentation.

---

## 4. Discovery

Discovery is the process through which a participant identifies that a commitment exists or identifies information that may allow its interpretation.

Discovery can occur:

- inside one work;
- across multiple works;
- across editions;
- between comics and music;
- between digital and physical artifacts;
- through a participating platform;
- through campaign communications;
- through community activity.

This creates an ecosystem in which different pieces of content can function as parts of a larger information system.

---

## 5. Revelation

A revelation is the participant's presentation of the information or condition required by the campaign.

Examples include:

- revealing a committed value;
- identifying a hidden relationship;
- submitting a valid code;
- demonstrating a cross-content connection;
- producing a required sequence;
- identifying the correct edition or serial;
- presenting a cryptographic preimage or equivalent proof.

A campaign may define whether revelations are:

- public or private;
- individual or collective;
- open-ended or time-limited;
- simultaneous or sequential;
- mass or selective;
- first-valid-revelation based.

---

## 6. Verification

Verification determines whether the submitted revelation satisfies the documented campaign condition.

Verification should be reproducible and based on predefined rules.

A verification process may involve:

- identifier validation;
- serial validation;
- hash comparison;
- nonce validation;
- timestamp validation;
- cross-reference validation;
- campaign-state validation;
- cryptographic proof validation.

The technical cryptographic procedures should remain in the canonical **Cryptographic Infrastructure** documentation rather than being duplicated here.

---

## 7. Benefit

A valid revelation may activate a defined benefit.

Benefits can include:

- access;
- discounts;
- promotions;
- royalties;
- rewards;
- privileges;
- exclusive content;
- collectibles;
- participation rights;
- recognition;
- platform-specific benefits;
- future ecosystem opportunities.

The benefit must be defined before participation whenever the campaign requires a deterministic economic condition.

---

## 8. Redemption

Redemption is the final operational stage in which a verified participant receives or activates the corresponding benefit.

A campaign may define:

- who may redeem;
- what must be verified;
- redemption windows;
- redemption limits;
- whether a benefit is transferable;
- whether redemption is single-use;
- whether redemption is associated with an email, Bitcoin address, serial, or another identity mechanism;
- whether the first valid revelation has priority.

Redemption rules should be documented as part of the campaign specification.

---

# Campaign Patterns

## Mass Revelation

Any participant satisfying the conditions may qualify.

Example:

> Every participant who correctly reveals the committed value before the campaign deadline receives the defined benefit.

## Selective Revelation

Only a defined group, edition, platform, or participation class may qualify.

## Single Revelation

The campaign provides one benefit to the first valid participant, or another explicitly defined single-winner condition.

## First-Valid-Revelation

Multiple participants may attempt the revelation, but the campaign recognizes the first valid submission according to its documented verification rule.

These patterns are not rankings of participants. They are different campaign mechanics.

---

# Cross-Content Economy

The model becomes especially relevant when several creative works participate in the same economic system.

For example:

**Comic A → clue → Comic B → revelation condition → Song C → verification → benefit**

A participant may therefore need to move through several works or platforms to reconstruct the complete condition.

This allows the ecosystem to create economic relationships between:

- comics;
- music;
- collectibles;
- publications;
- websites;
- GitHub repositories;
- participating platforms;
- sponsors;
- distributors;
- physical events;
- future digital services.

The content itself becomes part of the participation infrastructure.

---

# Platform Participation

A platform or company may participate by hosting, distributing, sponsoring, or activating part of a campaign.

Possible arrangements include:

- exclusive content;
- platform-specific revelations;
- distribution partnerships;
- sponsorship campaigns;
- co-branded campaigns;
- revenue sharing;
- royalty participation;
- redemption infrastructure;
- identity or access services.

The participating platform does not need to control the entire ecosystem. Its role can be limited to a documented part of the campaign.

---

# Economic Principle

The central economic principle is:

> **A participant creates economic value by discovering, revealing, and verifying information associated with creative content.**

This creates an alternative relationship between creator, content, participant, and business partner.

Instead of treating content exclusively as something to consume, the model can treat content as a structured field of participation.

---

# Ecosystem Role

The Commitment & Revelation Economy is intended to connect the creative layer of the Trilema Ecosystem with its business infrastructure.

It can serve:

- creators;
- writers;
- artists;
- musicians;
- collectors;
- sponsors;
- distributors;
- platforms;
- participants;
- developers;
- researchers;
- future ecosystem operators.

The model is deliberately reusable so that individual works can maintain their own identity while participating in a common economic architecture.
