# Trilema Institute — Business Infrastructure

## Explore Business Infrastructure

**Business Infrastructure** is the business, economic, participation, partnership, distribution, identity, campaign, and verification coordination layer of the **Trilema Ecosystem by LAEV**.

It is designed to provide a structured framework through which creative works, digital content, collectibles, participants, platforms, sponsors, collaborators, and future ecosystem services can interact through documented and reusable business models.

The purpose of this infrastructure is not to turn every creative work into the same product. Instead, it establishes a common business architecture while allowing each work, project, campaign, platform, or collaboration to retain its own identity.

---

## 01 — Trilema Ecosystem

The first layer defines the general economic architecture of the ecosystem.

It covers:

- distributed creation;
- ecosystem participation;
- platform participation;
- content distribution;
- economic participation;
- business model development;
- coordination between independent creative and technical projects.

This layer answers a fundamental question:

> **How can independent participants and projects become economically connected without requiring every component of the ecosystem to become the same entity?**

The ecosystem can therefore operate as a network of related works, contributors, platforms, campaigns, and infrastructure.

---

## 02 — Commitment & Revelation Economy

The **Commitment & Revelation Economy** is a central business mechanism of the infrastructure.

The basic concept is:

**Creation → Participation → Commitment → Discovery → Revelation → Verification → Benefit → Redemption**

A commitment can be embedded or associated with content before its meaning or condition is publicly revealed.

A participant may discover information distributed across different works, platforms, editions, campaigns, or other ecosystem components and later demonstrate a valid revelation.

The infrastructure then provides a framework for:

- defining commitments;
- defining revelation conditions;
- connecting information across content;
- verifying valid revelations;
- establishing proof of revelation;
- defining benefits;
- defining redemption conditions.

This creates an economic relationship between **content discovery and participation**.

---

## 03 — Partnership Models

The partnership layer defines how external organizations, platforms, creators, sponsors, distributors, and collaborators can participate.

Models include:

- sponsorship;
- licensing;
- distribution;
- exclusive content;
- co-production;
- revenue sharing;
- royalties.

The objective is to make partnerships describable as reusable models rather than isolated agreements invented from zero for every project.

A campaign may therefore select an appropriate partnership model while retaining its own specific terms.

---

## 04 — Campaign Models

Campaigns provide a practical way to activate the infrastructure.

The framework supports different participation and redemption configurations, including:

- mass participation;
- selective participation;
- single participation;
- mass redemption;
- selective redemption;
- single redemption;
- first-valid-revelation mechanisms.

This allows different campaigns to use different economic rules while relying on the same underlying infrastructure.

For example, one campaign could reward many valid participants, while another could define a limited number of valid revelations.

---

## 05 — Content & Collectibles

This layer connects the business infrastructure to the actual creative material.

It defines models for:

- narrative content;
- collectibles;
- cross-content relationships;
- platform-native content;
- reality-linked content.

The important distinction is that **content remains content**, while the infrastructure defines the mechanisms through which content can participate in an economic or interactive system.

A comic, recording, artwork, publication, collectible, digital artifact, or future media format can therefore become a component of a larger campaign without losing its independent identity.

---

## 06 — Identity & Serials

A business infrastructure requires deterministic identification.

The identity layer establishes canonical identifiers for:

- works;
- issues;
- editions;
- versions;
- serials;
- campaigns;
- platforms;
- revelations.

This provides a common reference system for the ecosystem.

The principle is:

> **One concept → one canonical identity → multiple references.**

Identifiers allow different parts of the ecosystem to refer to the same object without creating competing definitions.

---

## 07 — Cryptographic Infrastructure

The cryptographic layer provides the technical foundation for commitments and verification.

It covers:

- commitments;
- hashing;
- nonces;
- randomness;
- timestamping;
- verification protocols;
- Bitcoin anchoring.

This layer is deliberately separated from the economic model.

The business infrastructure defines **what must be proven and what the economic consequence is**.

The cryptographic infrastructure defines **how the relevant information can be committed, revealed, timestamped, and verified**.

---

## 08 — Campaign Specification

Campaign specifications transform the conceptual models into structured definitions.

Schemas are provided for:

- campaigns;
- participation;
- commitments;
- revelations;
- benefits;
- redemption.

The purpose is to make campaigns reproducible and machine-readable in future implementations.

A campaign can therefore be described as a defined configuration of existing infrastructure rather than as an entirely new system.

---

## 09 — Roadmap

The roadmap defines the progressive development of the business infrastructure.

The intended approach is incremental:

1. define the concepts;
2. document the canonical models;
3. establish identities and serials;
4. define cryptographic verification;
5. define campaign specifications;
6. test participation and redemption models;
7. develop partnerships and platform integrations;
8. expand toward future ecosystem services.

The roadmap can evolve as the ecosystem develops.

---

# The Canonical Documentation Principle

The infrastructure follows a strict documentation principle:

> **One concept → one canonical document → multiple references.**

This prevents the same business rule from being rewritten differently in multiple places.

For example, the canonical identity model belongs to:

**06 — Identity & Serials**

The cryptographic verification model belongs to:

**07 — Cryptographic Infrastructure**

Campaign definitions belong to:

**08 — Campaign Specification**

Other documents should reference these canonical definitions instead of duplicating them.

This creates a documentation architecture that can grow without continuously fragmenting the underlying concepts.
